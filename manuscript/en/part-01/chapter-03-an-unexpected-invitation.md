# Chapter 3 — An Unexpected Invitation

The following Wednesday, Emily discovered that Michael Reed had a talent for making ordinary school days unpredictable.

It began with a missing notebook.

Not hers this time.

His.

He stood beside their classroom desk, searching through his backpack with the concentration of a detective investigating a serious crime.

Emily watched him for a moment.

“Lost something?”

“My literature notes.”

“Again?”

He looked up.

“Why does everyone assume I'm irresponsible?”

“Because you keep losing things.”

“I don't lose them. I temporarily misplace them.”

“That's a very sophisticated way of losing something.”

Michael placed both hands on the desk.

“I believe you're enjoying this.”

“Only a little.”

He searched his backpack again.

Then he stopped.

“Wait.”

He reached into the side pocket and pulled out a folded sheet of paper.

Emily stared at him.

“Was that difficult?”

“Extremely. I almost had to think.”

She laughed.

He unfolded the paper.

It was their presentation outline, covered in notes and corrections.

“You kept the original,” she said.

“Of course.”

“I thought you had lost it.”

“I told you. Temporary misplacement.”

Emily shook her head.

“I'm beginning to understand why your teachers look tired.”

Michael smiled.

“It's a gift.”

***

Their presentation was beginning to take shape.

They had agreed to meet twice a week, sometimes in the library and sometimes in the small café near the school.

The meetings were supposed to last an hour.

They rarely did.

Not because they were inefficient.

Quite the opposite.

They worked well together. Emily organized their ideas, checked the references, and made sure every section connected logically. Michael handled much of the presentation design and had a surprising ability to explain complicated ideas in simple language.

But somewhere between discussing books and arguing over the best title slide, they had started talking about everything else.

Music.

Movies.

Family holidays.

The places they wanted to visit.

The kind of people they hoped to become.

Emily discovered that Michael wanted to study engineering.

He liked understanding how things worked. He was fascinated by bridges, buildings, and machines, and he often wondered why certain designs succeeded while others failed.

Michael discovered that Emily loved literature, photography, and old buildings.

She liked places that carried stories.

“Why old buildings?” he asked one afternoon.

“Because they have seen things.”

“Buildings?”

“Yes.”

“What exactly have they seen?”

“People. Families. Weddings. Arguments. Birthdays. People leaving and people coming back.”

Michael looked at her.

“You make buildings sound like elderly relatives.”

Emily laughed.

“Maybe they are.”

He glanced through the café window at an old brick building across the street.

“Then I suppose you would like a building that has a good memory.”

“I would.”

He nodded thoughtfully.

“I'll remember that.”

She smiled.

“You say that a lot.”

“Because I keep discovering things worth remembering.”

Emily looked down at her drink.

She was beginning to notice that Michael had a way of saying ordinary things that made them feel unexpectedly important.

***

Sarah noticed the change before Emily admitted it to herself.

They were sitting together in the cafeteria when Sarah put down her fork.

“So, how many times have you met him this week?”

Emily continued eating.

“Who?”

Sarah stared at her.

“Don't do that.”

“Do what?”

“Pretend you don't know who I'm talking about.”

Emily smiled.

“We're working on the presentation.”

“Of course. The presentation.”

Sarah leaned closer.

“Does the presentation require you to smile at your phone every time it lights up?”

Emily put her phone face down.

“I don't smile.”

“You just did.”

“Sarah.”

“Emily.”

They looked at each other.

Then both laughed.

Sarah softened her voice.

“I'm happy for you.”

Emily looked up.

“For what?”

“For whatever this is becoming.”

Emily became quiet.

“I don't know what it is.”

“That's fine.”

Sarah took another bite.

“You don't have to name everything immediately.”

Emily smiled.

It was one of the reasons she loved Sarah.

She could be dramatic, nosy, and occasionally impossible.

But when something mattered, she knew when to stop teasing.

Then Sarah added:

“Although, if you two get married, I expect to be the maid of honor.”

Emily nearly choked on her drink.

“Sarah!”

“What? I'm planning ahead.”

“You're impossible.”

“Thank you.”

***

That Friday, Michael invited Emily to join him and a few classmates after school.

They were planning to visit a small bookstore in the city before getting something to eat.

Sarah was supposed to come too, but she had a family commitment.

Emily hesitated when she read Michael's message.

**Michael:** A few of us are going to the bookstore after school. Want to come?

**Emily:** Who's going?

**Michael:** Daniel, Olivia, me. Maybe Ethan.

**Emily:** Sarah can't make it.

**Michael:** That's okay. You're still welcome.

She read the message again.

There was nothing unusual about it.

Still, she felt a small flutter of excitement.

**Emily:** All right. I'll come.

**Michael:** Excellent. I promise not to argue with any vending machines.

**Emily:** I can't promise I won't.

***

The bookstore was crowded.

It had narrow aisles, wooden shelves, and the pleasant smell of paper and coffee.

Emily loved it immediately.

Olivia Collins was already browsing the poetry section when they arrived. She was elegant, articulate, and always seemed to know something about whatever subject happened to come up.

Daniel was looking for a technical book.

Ethan was trying to convince Michael that a particular science-fiction novel was the greatest book ever written.

Emily wandered toward the literature shelves.

Michael followed.

“Looking for something specific?” he asked.

“Not really.”

“That's dangerous.”

“Why?”

“You might leave with six books.”

Emily smiled.

“That's not dangerous. That's a successful afternoon.”

He laughed.

They spent several minutes browsing.

Michael picked up a book with an elaborate cover.

“Have you read this?”

“No.”

“Would you recommend it?”

“I can't recommend something I haven't read.”

“Fair enough.”

He turned the book over.

“Then I'll buy it and report back.”

Emily raised an eyebrow.

“You're going to buy a book because I haven't read it?”

“I'm buying it because it looks interesting.”

“Of course.”

He smiled.

Then he noticed a photography book on the shelf beside her.

“Is this your kind of thing?”

Emily took it from him.

Inside were photographs of cities, old streets, and people going about their daily lives.

She turned a few pages.

“I love pictures like these.”

“Why?”

“Because they capture moments that would otherwise disappear.”

Michael looked at the open page.

A photograph showed an elderly couple sitting together on a park bench.

He studied it for a moment.

“They look happy.”

“They do.”

“Do you think they know someone took their picture?”

“Probably not.”

He smiled.

“Maybe that's what makes it beautiful.”

Emily looked at him.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Michael reached for the book.

“Come on. I'll get it for you.”

“No, Michael. You don't have to.”

“I know.”

“I can buy it myself.”

“I know that too.”

He held it out to her.

“Consider it a thank-you for the literature assignment.”

Emily hesitated.

Then she accepted it.

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome.”

She looked down at the photograph again.

It was the first book he ever gave her.

Neither of them called it a gift.

Not yet.

***

They left the bookstore just before sunset.

The others had decided to walk toward a nearby restaurant.

Emily was standing outside, adjusting her scarf, when a car passed too close to the curb and splashed water from a puddle onto the sidewalk.

She stepped back quickly.

Michael reached for her arm.

“Careful.”

She looked down at her shoes.

A few drops had landed on the edge of one.

“Wonderful.”

Michael examined the pavement.

“That driver clearly has a personal problem with pedestrians.”

Emily laughed.

“Don't start a diplomatic dispute with the traffic.”

“I make no promises.”

He removed his scarf and offered it to her.

She looked at it.

“Why?”

“Your sleeve is wet.”

“It's fine.”

“Emily.”

She looked up.

He was smiling, but there was a quiet seriousness in his expression.

“Take it.”

She accepted the scarf.

It was warm.

And it smelled faintly of his cologne.

They walked toward the restaurant together.

Behind them, Daniel called out that they were moving too slowly.

Michael turned.

“Some of us appreciate the scenery!”

Emily laughed.

She did not return the scarf until they reached the station.

***

At the restaurant, Olivia was telling a story about a university open day she had attended with her older sister.

Daniel interrupted twice.

Ethan disagreed with almost everything.

Michael listened, occasionally adding a joke that sent the table into laughter.

Emily watched him.

He was comfortable with people.

He did not dominate the conversation, but he knew how to make others feel included.

When Olivia asked Emily about her plans after school, Michael turned toward her and listened.

“I'd like to study literature,” Emily said. “Maybe something connected to publishing or writing.”

“That's interesting,” Olivia replied. “Have you thought about journalism?”

“Maybe. I haven't decided.”

Michael nodded.

“You'd be good at it.”

Emily looked at him.

“Why?”

“You notice things.”

She smiled.

“That's not necessarily a qualification.”

“It should be.”

He returned to his meal.

Emily looked down at her plate, trying to hide the smile that had appeared on her face.

***

When the evening ended, Michael walked Emily to the station.

The others had already left.

The platform was busy, and the digital board showed that her train would arrive in six minutes.

Michael stood beside her.

“Did you have a good time?”

“Yes. Thank you for inviting me.”

“I'm glad you came.”

She adjusted the strap of her bag.

“Me too.”

A short silence followed.

Then he pointed toward the book in her hand.

“Tell me if you like the photographs.”

“I will.”

“And if you don't, I'll pretend I never recommended it.”

“You didn't recommend it. You bought it.”

“Exactly. That's a much bigger commitment.”

Emily laughed.

Her train appeared in the distance.

She turned toward him.

“I should go.”

“See you Monday?”

“Of course.”

She stepped toward the train doors.

Then she turned back.

“Michael?”

“Yes?”

“Thank you for the scarf.”

He looked down at it, still around her shoulders.

“You can return it Monday.”

She smiled.

“I know.”

The doors opened.

She boarded and found a place near the window.

As the train began to move, she looked outside.

Michael was still standing on the platform.

He raised his hand.

She raised hers.

Then the station disappeared behind her.

Emily settled into her seat and opened the photography book.

On the first page, Michael had written something in small handwriting.

*For Emily — who notices the moments everyone else walks past.*

She read it once.

Then again.

And this time, she did not try to hide her smile.
