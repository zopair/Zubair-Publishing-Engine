# Chapter 6 — The Exhibition of Small Things

The school exhibition occupied the main hall, the library corridor, and nearly every available wall that could support a sheet of paper.

Students had spent weeks preparing displays for art, science, literature, photography, and design. Teachers moved between tables with clipboards, trying to look organized while answering questions from parents who had arrived early.

Emily came with Sarah.

Sarah stopped just inside the entrance.

“It's bigger than I expected.”

“It's the same hall we use for assemblies.”

“Yes, but now it has ambition.”

Emily laughed.

They collected a programme and began walking through the displays.

Sarah paused at a painting of a city street.

“I like this.”

Emily studied it. “The light is good.”

“That's what you would say.”

“What would you say?”

“That the people look like they're all late for something.”

Emily smiled. “They do.”

They moved on.

Emily found Michael's design project near the far wall. It was a model of a pedestrian bridge, built from wood and thin metal supports. Beside it stood a set of drawings showing different structural approaches.

Michael was speaking with a teacher.

He wore a dark blue shirt with the sleeves rolled to his elbows. His hair looked as though he had tried to arrange it and then abandoned the effort.

When he saw Emily, his face brightened.

“You came.”

“I said I would.”

“I know. I just wasn't sure whether you'd remember.”

Sarah looked at him.

“She remembers everything.”

Emily nudged her.

Michael smiled.

“Good to know.”

He turned toward the model.

“This is the final version. The first one collapsed.”

“Why?”

“I made the supports too narrow.”

Sarah leaned closer.

“Was that intentional?”

“It's called testing.”

“Was the collapse intentional?”

“Less intentional.”

Emily laughed.

Michael explained the design, showing how the weight moved through the structure and how the supports distributed pressure. He spoke with the same quiet enthusiasm she had heard when he talked about bridges in the library.

She listened carefully.

“It's beautiful,” she said.

He looked surprised.

“It's a bridge.”

“Those aren't mutually exclusive.”

Sarah nodded.

“She's right. It looks like something people would actually want to cross.”

Michael's smile grew.

“Thank you.”

A teacher approached and asked him to explain the model to another group. He glanced at Emily.

“I'll be back.”

“Take your time.”

As he moved away, Sarah leaned toward her.

“That's a very good sign.”

“What is?”

“He looks for you when he finishes talking to people.”

Emily pretended to study the model.

“Maybe he wants feedback.”

“Of course.”

***

The photography display was in the library corridor.

Emily had submitted six photographs, each printed and mounted on a simple white board. She had chosen ordinary scenes: a woman holding a paper bag at a bus stop, sunlight across an apartment staircase, an empty bench after rain, a shopkeeper opening his shutters, and two children sharing an umbrella.

The sixth was a picture of an old doorway.

Sarah stopped in front of it.

“This one is my favorite.”

“Why?”

“I don't know. It looks like someone is about to come home.”

Emily looked at the photograph again.

She had taken it on a quiet street after visiting the library. She had liked the worn blue paint and the small brass number beside the door.

“I think I know what you mean.”

A woman standing nearby turned toward them.

“Did you take these?”

Emily nodded.

“They're lovely.”

“Thank you.”

The woman introduced herself as one of the local photographers who had been invited to judge the exhibition. She asked Emily about the doorway photograph.

Emily explained that she liked places that seemed to hold traces of the people who lived in them.

The judge studied the image.

“You have a good eye for absence.”

Emily was not sure what to say.

The woman smiled.

“That's not a criticism. Sometimes what's missing is what makes a photograph speak.”

After she moved on, Sarah nudged Emily.

“You're going to be famous.”

“Please don't.”

“Fine. You'll be modestly successful.”

“That's better.”

***

Michael returned just as Emily was explaining the bus-stop photograph to another student.

He waited until the conversation ended.

“I didn't know you were displaying your work.”

“It's not much.”

He looked at the photographs.

“Emily.”

“What?”

“These are really good.”

She felt her cheeks warm.

“Thank you.”

He stopped in front of the photograph of the empty bench.

“Where was this?”

“Near the river. It had rained earlier.”

“Why did you take it?”

“I liked the way the water had collected on the seat. It looked like someone had just left.”

Michael studied the image.

“Or someone was about to arrive.”

Emily looked at him.

“That's another way to see it.”

He nodded.

“I think I like that better.”

Sarah looked between them.

“I'm going to find the refreshments.”

Emily turned. “You don't have to—”

“I know.”

Sarah walked away with a smile.

Michael watched her go.

“She seems to enjoy leaving us alone.”

“She enjoys making things awkward.”

“Is it working?”

Emily laughed.

“Sometimes.”

They stood together in front of the photographs.

Michael pointed to the doorway.

“This one reminds me of your house.”

“How?”

“The light in the hallway. The old frame around the mirror.”

Emily had not expected him to remember.

“You noticed that?”

“Of course.”

She smiled.

There it was again: his habit of keeping small things.

***

Later, the students gathered in the main hall for the exhibition announcements.

Emily stood with Sarah near the back. Michael was beside Daniel, who had spent the afternoon explaining a science project involving a small motor and a remarkably noisy fan.

The photography judge announced a special mention for Emily's collection.

For a moment, Emily did not realize she had heard her name.

Sarah grabbed her arm.

“That's you.”

Emily walked forward, feeling the room turn toward her.

The judge handed her a small certificate and said a few words about her ability to find meaning in ordinary scenes.

Emily thanked her.

When she returned, Sarah hugged her.

“I told you.”

“You told me I'd be famous.”

“I'm still right.”

Michael approached after the announcements.

“Congratulations.”

“Thank you.”

He looked genuinely pleased.

“I liked the one of the bench.”

“I know.”

“And the doorway.”

“You said that already.”

“I wanted to say it again.”

She smiled.

Daniel joined them.

“Congratulations, Emily. You made the rest of us look unprepared.”

“Your fan nearly took flight,” Michael said.

“It was a prototype.”

“It was a noise with ambition.”

Sarah laughed.

The four of them began walking toward the refreshments.

***

Near the entrance, Emily noticed Olivia Collins speaking with a group of students. She looked polished as usual, wearing a cream jacket and carrying a small notebook.

When she saw Michael, she approached.

“Michael, your bridge model was impressive.”

“Thank you.”

“I liked the way you explained the load distribution. It made the design much easier to understand.”

He nodded. “I'm glad.”

Olivia turned to Emily.

“Your photographs were beautiful. I especially liked the old doorway.”

“Thank you.”

“I think you have a strong sense of composition.”

Emily smiled. “That's kind of you.”

Olivia looked back at Michael.

“Are you going to the student design forum next month? I heard they're looking for volunteers.”

“I haven't decided.”

“You should. It would suit you.”

“Maybe.”

Olivia spoke with confidence and genuine interest. She was not merely making conversation; she had questions about the model and suggestions for how Michael might develop it.

Emily listened, appreciating her knowledge even as she became aware of a small, unexpected feeling in her chest.

It was not anger.

Not exactly jealousy.

More like the sudden realization that Michael had a life full of people, possibilities, and conversations that did not include her.

The thought unsettled her.

She stepped away to get a drink.

***

Sarah found her near the refreshments table.

“You okay?”

“Of course.”

Sarah followed her gaze toward Olivia and Michael.

“Oh.”

“Don't.”

“I haven't said anything.”

“You're thinking loudly.”

Sarah poured herself some lemonade.

“Olivia likes smart people. That's hardly a crime.”

“I didn't say it was.”

“And Michael is allowed to talk to people.”

“I know.”

Sarah looked at her carefully.

“Then what is bothering you?”

Emily took a breath.

“I don't know. I suppose I just realized I don't know what he thinks about me.”

Sarah's expression softened.

“That's different from knowing he doesn't like you.”

Emily looked down at her cup.

“I know.”

“Don't turn an unanswered question into a bad answer.”

Emily smiled faintly.

“Since when did you become wise?”

“Since you started needing it.”

They laughed.

Across the hall, Michael glanced toward them.

When he saw Emily, he excused himself from the conversation and came over.

“Everything all right?”

“Yes,” Emily said.

He looked at her for a moment.

“Are you sure?”

She nodded.

He did not press her.

Instead, he held out a small paper plate.

“I saved you one of the chocolate biscuits. The good ones.”

Emily looked at it.

“You remembered.”

“You said last week that the plain ones were disappointing.”

She accepted the plate.

“They are.”

“I agree.”

She looked at him, and the small discomfort inside her began to loosen.

***

The exhibition was nearly over when Michael asked whether Emily wanted to walk outside.

The evening air was cool. The school grounds were quieter now, with only a few parents carrying boxes toward their cars.

They stopped beside the old brick wall near the entrance.

“Did you enjoy today?” he asked.

“Yes. I was nervous about the photographs.”

“You shouldn't have been.”

“Easy for you to say. Your bridge didn't have to stand in front of a room full of people.”

“It had to stand in front of a room full of people who could see if it collapsed.”

Emily laughed.

“That's worse.”

“Exactly.”

They stood in comfortable silence.

Then Michael said, “I liked what you said about the doorway.”

“What did I say?”

“That places hold traces of people.”

Emily thought for a moment.

“I think they do. Even when people leave, something remains.”

He looked toward the school building.

“Maybe that's true of more than places.”

She turned toward him.

“Maybe.”

A car pulled up near the entrance. Sarah waved from the passenger seat of her mother's car.

Emily waved back.

“I should go.”

Michael nodded.

“Will I see you Monday?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

She took a step, then stopped.

“Michael?”

“Yeah?”

“I'm glad you invited me today.”

His smile was quiet.

“I'm glad you came.”

She walked toward the car.

Sarah leaned across the passenger seat as Emily climbed in.

“Did you have a nice walk?”

Emily fastened her seat belt.

“Yes.”

Sarah smiled.

“Good.”

The car pulled away.

Emily looked through the rear window.

Michael was still standing by the brick wall, his hands in his pockets, watching the car leave.

She turned forward again.

For the first time, she allowed herself to wonder whether the feeling between them might be becoming something neither of them had planned.
